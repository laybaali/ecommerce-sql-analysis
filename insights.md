# Key Findings & Business Insights

## 1. Revenue is Highly Concentrated Geographically

São Paulo alone contributes  ~37.47% of total revenue , followed by:
- Rio de Janeiro:  ~13.39% 
- Minas Gerais:  ~11.70% 

This indicates that a significant portion of revenue is concentrated in a small number of states.

 Business Implication 
- Strong performance in core markets provides stability  
- However, this creates  dependency risk and growth opportunity   
- Lower-performing states should be evaluated for:
  - Untapped demand  
  - Logistics constraints  
  - Category availability  
  - Regional pricing strategies  



## 2. Customer Retention is the Largest Growth Opportunity

- Total customers:  ~96,096 
- Repeat customers:  ~2,997 
- Repeat rate:  ~3.12% 

The vast majority ( ~97% ) of customers make only one purchase.

 Business Implication 
- The business is heavily  acquisition-driven 
- Improving  second-purchase conversion  is a major opportunity

 Potential Strategies 
- Post-purchase engagement
- Personalized recommendations
- Second-order incentives
- Cross-selling
- Loyalty programs
- Re-engagement campaigns



## 3. Repeat Customers are Significantly More Valuable

| Customer Type | Avg. Customer Value |
|--|-|
| One-time     | 161.82            |
| Repeat       | 314.99            |

Repeat customers generate  ~2× higher value per customer .

 Business Implication 
- Retention is not just a volume issue, it's a  revenue multiplier 
- Even small improvements in retention can significantly increase revenue



## 4. Revenue Still Depends on One-Time Customers

- One-time customers contribute  ~94.1% of total revenue 
- Repeat customers contribute only  ~5.9% 

 Insight 
- Revenue is currently driven by  scale (acquisition)  rather than  loyalty 

 Business Implication 
- The business already has a large customer base  
- Converting even a fraction into repeat buyers could unlock  substantial revenue growth 



## 5. Customer Value is Highly Uneven

- Average customer value:  ~166.59 
- Maximum observed value:  ~13,664 

Some customers generate high revenue even with a single purchase.

 Business Implication 
- Customer value ≠ purchase frequency only  
- Segmentation should consider:
  - Monetary value  
  - Purchase behavior  



## 6. RFM Segmentation Reveals Actionable Customer Groups

Identified segments:
- Champions
- Loyal Customers
- Potential Loyalists
- At Risk High Value
- Lost Customers

Key highlights:
-  Champions = highest average value (~382.30)
-  At Risk High Value :
  - ~14,932 customers  
  - ~4.70M revenue  

 Business Implication 
- “At Risk High Value” customers are critical:
  - High past spend  
  - Declining recent activity  
- Ideal target for  reactivation campaigns 



## 7. Seller Ecosystem Shows Moderate Concentration

- Total sellers:  ~3,095 
- Top seller revenue:  ~507K 
- Top 10 sellers contribute:  ~14.06% of revenue 

 Insight 
- Revenue is  not overly concentrated , but still influenced by top performers

 Seller Distribution 
- 18.45% - 1 order  
- 28.17% - 2–5 orders  
- 53.38% - 5+ orders  

 Business Implication 
Two clear opportunity segments:

 High-performing sellers 
- Retention
- Incentives
- Strategic partnerships

 Low-volume sellers 
- Activation
- Onboarding improvements
- Support & enablement



## 8. Logistics Performance is Strong but Not Uniform

- On-time deliveries:  ~92.13% 
- Delayed deliveries:  ~7.87% 
- Average delay:  ~8.87 days 

 Insight 
- Operational performance is strong overall  
- However, delays are  non-trivial in duration 



## 9. Delays are Associated with Higher-Value Orders

| Delivery Status | Avg. Order Value |
|-|-:|
| On Time        | 158.72         |
| Delayed        | 172.73         |

Delayed orders are  ~9% higher in value .

 Business Implication 
- Delays disproportionately affect  higher-value transactions 
- This increases risk to:
  - Customer satisfaction  
  - Retention  
  - Brand perception  



## 10. Multi-Seller Orders Are Rare but Interesting

- Single-seller orders:  ~98.70% 
- Multi-seller orders:  ~1.30% 

 Insight 
- Marketplace complexity is limited in practice  
- Multi-seller fulfillment is not a dominant pattern  

 Important Observation 
- Multi-seller orders showed  lower delay rates  than expected  
- Suggests that seller count alone does not drive delivery performance  



# Business Recommendations

## 1. Improve Customer Retention
- Focus on second-purchase conversion
- Use targeted engagement and incentives
- Build loyalty mechanisms

## 2. Reactivate High-Value At-Risk Customers
- Prioritize RFM-based targeting
- Use personalized campaigns instead of generic offers

## 3. Expand Beyond Core Geographic Markets
- Investigate low-revenue states
- Address supply, logistics, or demand gaps

## 4. Strengthen Seller Ecosystem
- Support top sellers strategically
- Improve activation of low-performing sellers

## 5. Investigate High-Value Delivery Delays
Focus analysis on:
- Product categories
- Sellers
- Regions
- Heavy/large products



# Project Takeaway

This project demonstrates how SQL can be used to solve real business problems across:

- Revenue performance  
- Customer behavior  
- Marketplace dynamics  
- Operational efficiency  

### Core Insight

The biggest growth opportunity is customer retention, while the key operational risk lies in high-value delivery delays.

Improving these two areas can significantly enhance both:
- Revenue growth  
- Customer experience
